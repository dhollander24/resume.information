The South Side Over a Decade: Data, Change, and the Reputation-Reality Gap ↗
Apr 2026 — DSA400W

Co-authored capstone data analysis examining whether Pittsburgh's South Side lives up to its reputation as dangerous and chaotic. Synthesized ten years (2016–2026) of crime incident data, housing violations, development permits, air quality indices, and community center attendance to test the gap between neighborhood reputation and lived reality. Key findings: crime has declined sharply and peaks during daytime hours — not weekend nights; property crime dominates (not violence); housing violations hit a decade high of 21,500 in 2024; air quality improved 50%; and community center attendance collapsed 87% from pre-pandemic levels. Analysis explicitly accounts for COVID-19 as a data artifact rather than a true trend. Concludes that the South Side is a neighborhood experiencing contradictory changes simultaneously — not a simple story in either direction.

Ames Housing Price Analysis — Multivariate Regression ↗
2025

Python-based regression analysis of 1,460 homes from the Ames Housing Dataset. Built and compared simple and multiple linear regression models (pandas, scikit-learn, seaborn) to predict sale price from square footage, bathroom count, bedroom count, and year built. Achieved R² of ~0.65 with the multi-feature model. Key insight: bedroom count shows positive correlation with price in isolation but a negative coefficient when square footage is controlled — a clear demonstration of multicollinearity. Included full train/test split, residual analysis, and feature importance visualization.
